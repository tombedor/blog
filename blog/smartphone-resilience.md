One of my favorite genres of corporate silliness is the upside-down pitch: an ad for a fatally flawed product in which the pitch seeks to convince the fatal flaw is actually the thing that's good about it.

The debut of Snapchat's smart glasses last week gave us a new entry.

The pitch for smart glasses goes like this: Smart phones are too distraction machines. They distract you from what's important in your life even when they are in your pocket <!-- link to research -->. What you _really_ want is smart glasses, which enable you to experience your life... while also having a screen in front of your face all the time?

Unfortunately for Snapchat, Meta, and Apple, would be customers have eyes. Spiegal's interview about how his new smart specs help you stay engaged with real life conversations was undermined by the ludicrously large, terminally distracting, uncomfortable looking googles on his face.

Spiegal is not unique in looking to displace the iPhone. It's a club he shares with Mark Zuckerberg, Jeff Bezos, and Sam Altman. And it's not hard to see why.
