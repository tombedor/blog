Sora is dead. Is this a temporary setback on the road to AI dominance of creative fields, or is there something more fundamental at play? *Can AI be creative at all?*

I don't think so. But let's clarify terms.

## What defines creativity?

The definition of creativity is a topic of academic debate. [Runco and Jaeger](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254301596_The_Standard_Definition_of_Creativity) state that in addition to being original, a creative work must be _effective_, demonstrating *"usefulness, fit, or appropriateness."*.

In "Creativity and Culture", Stein was more explicit about the role of an audience. For Stein, to be creative a work must be "accepted as tenable or useful or satisfying by a group at some point in time."

Is an audience required? Can a work really be useful if no one sees it? The work of some great artists was discovered only after their death: One of my favorite albums, [Pink Moon by Nick Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Moon), was only discovered 30 years after the songwriter's death. Was it not creative until then?

Stein's definition resonates with me. Up until after college, I was in a series of bands. While creating and playing songs with my friends was rewarding and fun, it was only when we got to perform in front of an audience that I felt real satisfaction from the creative work. So we'll go with Stein!

So with that in mind, how is AI doing?

## Originality and Scarcity

AI "creators" face a similar challenge to humans: there's vast surplus of content to choose from, and breaking through is quite difficult. To take music as an example, the average consumer's available library has grown exponentially, and is enough to listen to something different every second for X years.

## Platform behavior

Platforms have shown a conflicted relationship with AI: they need content, and in some cases are providing AI creation tools themselves. But much of what _is_ being produced is low quality or fraudulent.

Thus they have not _banned_ AI contributions, but are actively taking them down.
