Debate about whether the explosion of datacenter buildout will prove to be a worthwhile investment centers on two scenarios:

1. AI adoption accelerates, the datacenter investment pays out
1. AI adoption is not as fast as forecasted, and it doesn't.

However, a third scenario is very plausible:

_Open source models running on local workstations dominate AI_

There are a few reasons this could happen:


## Reasons the future of AI will be local

![scenarios](/diagrams/open-source-models/scenarios.png)

### Remote providers increase prices

There aren't reliable, public figures on the unit economics of Anthropic and OpenAI, but there are reasons to suspect they are unsustainable.

Cursor recently estimated a $200/month Claude Max subscription [can consume up to $5,000 in compute](https://www.forbes.com/sites/annatong/2026/03/05/cursor-goes-to-war-for-ai-coding-dominance/). Granted, these figures come from a rival, but there are other signs the big providers are looking to increase prices and/or focus on enterprise (read: customers willing to pay more).

The newly release [Claude Code Review](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review) comes priced at an eye-watering [$15-$25 per PR](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review#pricing). The release came with little information as to why it should replace existing PR review workflows. It seems as though this is a pricing experiment, to see how high a price enterprise is willing to tolerate.

OpenAI is also [reportedly narrowing its focus to enterprise](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-chatgpt-side-projects-16b3a825?st=oHmik2&reflink=article_copyURL_share)[^1].

[^1]: Granted, part of this seems to be motivated by some bets just not getting adoption, like the Sora video generation app.


## Open source models keep up

With the exception of gpt-4, open source models have matched performance of frontier models within 6 months of frontier model release ([data](#open-source-parity-data)):

<ParityChart />

Naturally, there have been accusations of open source models gaming evals, but the same could be argued of frontier models.

We can expect this to continue. Startups usually try to create a moat, but model providers build waterslides: frontier models help train their open source competitors.

Providers can ([and have](https://fortune.com/2026/02/24/anthropic-china-deepseek-theft-claude-distillation-copyright-national-security/)) complain about competitors using their model to train competition. As a practical matter, however, this "theft" could be impossible to prevent - and that's to say nothing of the [ask forgiveness rather than permission approach to intellectual property the providers have taken themselves](https://www.anthropiccopyrightsettlement.com/).

### Small, specialized models emerge

Given today's low prices, there seems to be little downward economic pressure on token usage. People reach for the most recent model, regardless of what they are doing.

This could change as price increases, and the increasing prevalence of subagent-driven workflows provide a natural transition. I probably don't need a frontier model to fix style issues in my Python PR - a small, specialized model can fill that void. If frontier models get dramatically more (i.e. $25 per PR review) expensive, demand will increase for these models, and the open source community will be plenty able to meet it.

## Private and free is hard to beat

If they can gain parity with hosted alternatives, local open source models have a compelling value proposition: _fast, private, and free_. This possibility has not gotten much attention: no one stands to get mega-rich from them. But the threat to current leaders is a potent one.

### On-Device Model Size {#on-device-model-size}

**Definition:** "Max usable model" is the largest Q4-quantized model that fits in device RAM *and* runs at ≥8 tokens/second with an 8k context window — a threshold for a responsive conversational experience. It is `min(RAM-fit, speed-fit)`, where:

- **RAM-fit** = `RAM × 0.8 / 0.75` — usable RAM (80% of total) divided by bytes per parameter at Q4 (~0.75 bytes/param after overhead)
- **Speed-fit** = `(memory_bandwidth / 51.2 GB/s) × (baseline_speed / bits_per_weight) × target_t/s_factor` — scales from a reference of ~11B params at 8 t/s on a 51.2 GB/s device

For MoE models, RAM-fit applies to **total** parameters (all weights must be loaded); speed-fit applies to **active** parameters only.
