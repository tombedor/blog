# Research Brief: Internet Disruption Analogies for AI vs. Software Incumbents

## Travel Agents

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Agents controlled practical access to fare search and booking through CRS/GDS terminals, phone relationships, paper ticketing, and local storefront trust. Airlines still depended on agents: ~80–85% of U.S. airline tickets were booked through agencies in 1995.
- https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1995-04-05-fi-51205-story.html
- https://www.upi.com/Archives/1995/02/17/Travel-agents-fighting-commission-cap/8953792997200/

**Was the 1995 commission cut driven by the internet? Mostly no.** The first cap was a cost-cutting move before consumer online travel had meaningful scale. Delta was trying to cut $2B in operating costs by 1997, including $400M from marketing; commissions were a major airline expense, described contemporaneously as third-largest after payroll and fuel. Online booking existed only at the edge: Internet Travel Network launched in 1995; in 1996, less than 1% of airline tickets were sold online.
- https://www.deseret.com/1995/2/10/19158434/delta-slaps-lid-on-ticket-commissions/
- https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/1995/02/11/american-northwest-join-deltas-commission-cap/9afc36b3-d850-4a27-8290-d8ec65bb2dca/
- https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/speeding-tickets-internet-distribution-travel-industry
- Pull quotes:
  - "lower operating costs and stem its three-year flow of red ink" (Deseret News)
  - "third-largest expense for U.S. carriers, behind payroll and fuel" (Washington Post)

**Internet was the accelerant, not the initial cause:** Airline direct websites and OTAs later made commission elimination stick by lowering distribution costs. GAO: in 2002 airlines lost almost $10B and still spent $7B distributing tickets; airline websites cost less because they avoided travel-agent commissions, CRS/GDS booking fees, and reservation-agent labor.
- https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-03-749
- https://www.oig.dot.gov/sites/default/files/cr2000111.pdf
- Pull quote: "they paid over $7 billion to distribute tickets to consumers" (GAO)

**Disruption was two-step, not one:**
- 1995: Airlines cut commissions 10% → 5% (Delta first, Feb 9, 1995), capped at $50 RT / $25 OW; commissions were 60% of avg agent revenue
- 1996–98: Travelocity, Expedia, Priceline launched/scaled direct-to-consumer booking
- March 2002: 8 of 10 largest airlines cut base commission to zero
  - https://www.travelpulse.com/voices/opinions/twenty-years-after-the-commission-caps
  - https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/will-commission-cuts-kill-the-small-travel-agent/
  - https://news.microsoft.com/1996/10/22/microsoft-expedia-travel-services-debuts-on-the-web/
  - https://www.wired.com/1998/03/name-your-price-airline-tickets/

**Scale of collapse:**
- Peak ARC-accredited locations: ~36,000 (1995)
- Retail agency establishments: 23,000 (1997) → under 10,000 (2013) = **59% decline**
- BLS 2024 employment: ~65,700 travel agents (vs. ~124,000–340,000 at dot-com peak — sources conflict)
  - https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/travel-agents.htm
  - https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-decline-of-travel-agents

**Survivor path:** Pivoted to luxury/complex travel, membership fees, Virtuoso consortium. Different product, different customer.

**Pull quote:** *"Retail travel agency establishments fell 59% between 1997 and 2013."* (TravelPulse)

## Classified Ads / Newspapers

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Newspapers owned the local buyer/seller marketplace because classified discovery was bundled into print distribution. Scarcity worked in their favor: ad placement was paid, local, slow, and hard to search, but buyers already looked in the paper.

**Moat:** Bundle lock-in — classifieds were 40% of newspaper ad revenue, ~30% of total revenue (2000). Classifieds also carried high margins and subsidized newsroom economics.
- https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/142129/2000-2010-the-decade-that-killed-newspaper-clas.html
- https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/11138/ad-spending-in-newspapers-hit-487-billion-in-200.html
- Pull quote: "Classifieds used to be a mainstay of the newspaper business" (MediaPost)

**Collapse stats:**
- Classified ad revenue peaked 2000: **$19.6 billion**
- 2012: **$4.6 billion** — 77% drop in 12 years
- Craigslist (founded 1995) estimated to have cost newspapers **$5 billion** in classified revenue, 2000–2007 (UW economist Robert Seamans)
  - https://phys.org/news/2013-08-craigslist-newspapers-billions.html
  - https://www.minnpost.com/business/2014/02/how-craigslist-killed-newspapers-golden-goose/

**Peer-reviewed source:** "Impact of Online Competition on Local Newspapers: Evidence from the Introduction of Craigslist," *Review of Economic Studies* (2024)
- https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/92/3/1738/7665573

**Mechanism for software-AI analogy:** Craigslist did not need better journalism; it unbundled the profitable transaction layer from the newspaper bundle. The scarce asset became non-scarce: self-serve posting, search, and near-zero distribution cost.
- https://cepr.org/publications/dp16130
- https://web.stanford.edu/~gjmartin/papers/Craigslist_Final.pdf
- Pull quote: "separate the effect of competition for classified advertising from other changes" (CEPR)

## Cab Drivers / Taxi Medallions

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Taxi incumbency was legal and operational, not informational. Medallions created a capped right to street hails; dispatch radio networks handled pre-arranged rides; consumers had poor visibility into supply, wait time, price, driver quality, or route quality.
- https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/economic-analysis-taxicab-regulation/233832.pdf
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/taxis/

**Why medallions were introduced:** The Haas Act (1937) was a Depression-era response to taxi oversupply, weak driver earnings, congestion, and public disorder/service problems. NYC's Board of Aldermen capped licenses at 13,595 after finding that too many taxis produced depressed earnings and congested streets; as some owners stopped renewing, active medallions fell to 11,787 and stayed there until the first city auction in 1996.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2020/01/Taxi-Medallion-Task-Force-Report-Final.pdf
- https://www.schallerconsult.com/taxi/taxi2.htm
- https://hrlr.law.columbia.edu/hrlr-online/distressed-drivers-solving-the-new-york-city-taxi-medallion-debt-crisis/
- Pull quotes:
  - "depressed driver earnings and congested city streets" (NYC Taxi Medallion Task Force)
  - "violent protest and the demands for industry regulation increased" (Columbia Human Rights Law Review)

**Motivation drift:** The original policy goal was stabilization/order, but transferability plus a long-running cap converted licenses into appreciating assets. The Task Force says the cap and transferability "created a valuable asset"; Schaller emphasizes the "moratorium on the issuance" of new licenses.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2020/01/Taxi-Medallion-Task-Force-Report-Final.pdf
- https://www.schallerconsult.com/taxi/taxi2.htm
- Pull quote: "created a valuable asset by establishing a virtual monopoly" (NYC Taxi Medallion Task Force)

**Moat:** Regulation-created scarcity. NYC had 13,587 medallions in 2019; medallion prices captured expected rents from limited taxi supply and the exclusive street-hail right.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/taxis/

**Collapse signal:** NYC medallions averaged **$1.16M** in March 2014 for wheelchair-accessible corporate medallions; by Nov. 2019 average all-medallion price was **$164,518**.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/taxis/
- Pull quote: "the medallion secondary market was essentially 'frozen'" (NYC Taxi Medallion Task Force)

**Mechanism:** Uber was mobile software more than generic "internet": GPS, maps, app dispatch, stored payments, two-sided ratings, and price/supply matching let non-medallion drivers compete with taxis on convenience. The old scarcity asset stopped mapping cleanly to consumer access.
- https://time.com/3609114/taxi-medallion-prices-decline-uber-uber-lyft/
- https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2015/11/cab-medallion-owners-sue-nyc-blame-uber-for-ruining-business/

**Caveat:** Do not over-attribute the medallion crash to Uber alone. NYC's Task Force and later reporting also point to inflated medallion prices, aggressive lending, and city revenue incentives. Better framing: software exposed and bypassed a fragile regulatory scarcity model.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/taxis/
- https://hrlr.law.columbia.edu/hrlr-online/distressed-drivers-solving-the-new-york-city-taxi-medallion-debt-crisis/

## Stockbrokers

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Consumers accessed markets through human brokers by phone. Execution, research, advice, account access, and trust were bundled; commission pricing made small or frequent trades expensive.

**Moat:** Execution gatekeeping plus bundled advice/research — full-service trades commonly ran **$100–$300** in the late 1990s; discount brokers and online brokers compressed the execution component toward commodity pricing.
- https://www.cbsnews.com/news/how-to-be-an-e-trader/
- https://biz.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Finance/Introduction_to_Investments_%28Paiano%29/New_Page/Chapter_18%3A_Brokerages_Account_Types_and_Annuities/15.01%3A_New_Page
- Pull quotes:
  - "The answer is simple: cost." (CBS News)
  - "between $100 and $300 per trade" (CBS News)

**Mechanism:** E*TRADE (1991), Schwab online (1995, launched with $181.7B AUM) → trades at $10 or less

**Scale:** By early 2000s, 18 million online accounts in US; online equity trades ~25% of industry-wide volume
- https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2005/12/art2full.pdf
- https://peterjdesilva.com/a-short-history-of-the-discount-brokerage-industry/

**Pull quote:** *"Full-service firms continually lost market share to these upstart firms and have never fully recovered."*

**Survivor path:** Repositioned as wealth managers/advisors for high-net-worth clients; commodity transaction layer fully automated.

**Robinhood continuation:** Robinhood pushed the already-commoditized execution layer to zero-commission mobile trading; Schwab cut U.S. stock/ETF/options online commissions from $4.95 to $0 in Oct. 2019, pressuring the rest of the brokerage market. This is closer to "software attacks software-enabled incumbents" than to the original web-vs-phone broker shift.
- https://www.cnbc.com/2019/10/01/charles-schwab-is-eliminating-online-commissions-for-trading-in-us-stocks-and-etfs.html
- https://content.schwab.com/web/retail/public/about-schwab/schwab_annual_report_2019.pdf
- Pull quotes:
  - "So we removed that barrier." (Schwab 2019 annual report)
  - "from $4.95 to $0.00" (Schwab 2019 annual report)

## Encyclopedia Britannica

**Moat:** Curated authority at premium price (~$1,400–$2,000/set); door-to-door sales force

**Collapse:** Peak 1990: >100,000 sets/year → print discontinued 2012; by then **print = 1% of revenue**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica
- HBR 2013: https://hbr.org/2013/03/encyclopaedia-britannicas-president-on-killing-off-a-244-year-old-product

**Key dynamic:** Microsoft Encarta (mid-1990s) undercut on price first; Wikipedia (2001) made curation free and continuously updated. Not a feature battle — an economics battle.

## AI vs. Software Incumbents: Current Signal

**Mechanism:** AI agents perform tasks previously requiring humans with SaaS seats; per-seat pricing collapses when agents replace workers
- *"AI agents compress seat counts by 90%"* (MindStudio)
- Bloomberg projects subscription pricing: 60% → 30% of software pricing; outcome-based: 10% → 60%, over next decade
  - https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2026/saas-ai-agents.html
  - https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/saas-pricing-ai-agent-era

**Market signal:** Public SaaS EV/Revenue fell from 18–19x pandemic peak to 5.1x (March 2026); IGV software ETF down ~30% from Sep 2025 peak; ~$2T in market cap erased
- https://www.saastr.com/the-saas-rout-of-2026-is-even-worse-than-you-think-for-the-first-time-ever-software-now-trades-at-a-discount-to-the-sp-500/

**2025 bifurcation (SaaStr data):** Palantir +142%, Cloudflare +80%, MongoDB +70% vs. HubSpot −51%, Monday.com −36%, Salesforce −31% — infrastructure and AI-native won; horizontal seat-based lost
- https://www.saastr.com/the-performing-major-b2b-stocks-of-2025-what-the-ai-divide-tells-us-about-the-future-of-saas/

**Key structural difference from prior disruptions:** SaaS incumbents are software companies — can embed AI and become the AI product. Microsoft/SAP/Oracle survived cloud transition via same playbook. ServiceNow is the clearest "adapting incumbent" analog.
- https://www.bain.com/insights/will-agentic-ai-disrupt-saas-technology-report-2025/
- https://insights.flagshipadvisorypartners.com/will-ai-kill-the-b2b-software-industry

**Most exposed:** Horizontal workflow tools (task tracking, generic CRM, marketing automation). **Most protected:** ERP/financial/compliance systems with deterministic/regulatory moats; infrastructure (Stripe, Twilio, Snowflake).
