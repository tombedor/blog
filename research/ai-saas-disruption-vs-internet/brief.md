# Research Brief: Internet Disruption Analogies for AI vs. SaaS

---

## Travel Agents

**Disruption was two-step, not one:**
- 1995: Airlines cut commissions 10% → 5% (Delta first, Feb 9, 1995), capped at $50 RT / $25 OW; commissions were 60% of avg agent revenue
- 1995–97: Expedia, Travelocity, Priceline launched direct-to-consumer booking
- March 2002: 8 of 10 largest airlines cut base commission to zero
  - https://www.travelpulse.com/voices/opinions/twenty-years-after-the-commission-caps
  - https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/will-commission-cuts-kill-the-small-travel-agent/

**Scale of collapse:**
- Peak ARC-accredited locations: ~36,000 (1995)
- Retail agency establishments: 23,000 (1997) → under 10,000 (2013) = **59% decline**
- BLS 2024 employment: ~65,700 travel agents (vs. ~124,000–340,000 at dot-com peak — sources conflict)
  - https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/travel-agents.htm
  - https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/the-decline-of-travel-agents

**Survivor path:** Pivoted to luxury/complex travel, membership fees, Virtuoso consortium. Different product, different customer.

**Pull quote:** *"Retail travel agency establishments fell 59% between 1997 and 2013."* (TravelPulse)

## Classified Ads / Newspapers

**Moat:** Bundle lock-in — classifieds were 40% of newspaper ad revenue, ~30% of total revenue (2000)

**Collapse stats:**
- Classified ad revenue peaked 2000: **$19.6 billion**
- 2012: **$4.6 billion** — 77% drop in 12 years
- Craigslist (founded 1995) estimated to have cost newspapers **$5 billion** in classified revenue, 2000–2007 (UW economist Robert Seamans)
  - https://phys.org/news/2013-08-craigslist-newspapers-billions.html
  - https://www.minnpost.com/business/2014/02/how-craigslist-killed-newspapers-golden-goose/

**Peer-reviewed source:** "Impact of Online Competition on Local Newspapers: Evidence from the Introduction of Craigslist," *Review of Economic Studies* (2024)
- https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/92/3/1738/7665573

## Stockbrokers

**Moat:** Execution monopoly — trades ran $100–$150+ at full-service firms; consumers had no direct market access

**Mechanism:** E*TRADE (1991), Schwab online (1995, launched with $181.7B AUM) → trades at $10 or less

**Scale:** By early 2000s, 18 million online accounts in US; online equity trades ~25% of industry-wide volume
- https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2005/12/art2full.pdf
- https://peterjdesilva.com/a-short-history-of-the-discount-brokerage-industry/

**Pull quote:** *"Full-service firms continually lost market share to these upstart firms and have never fully recovered."*

**Survivor path:** Repositioned as wealth managers/advisors for high-net-worth clients; commodity transaction layer fully automated.

## Encyclopedia Britannica

**Moat:** Curated authority at premium price (~$1,400–$2,000/set); door-to-door sales force

**Collapse:** Peak 1990: >100,000 sets/year → print discontinued 2012; by then **print = 1% of revenue**
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica
- HBR 2013: https://hbr.org/2013/03/encyclopaedia-britannicas-president-on-killing-off-a-244-year-old-product

**Key dynamic:** Microsoft Encarta (mid-1990s) undercut on price first; Wikipedia (2001) made curation free and continuously updated. Not a feature battle — an economics battle.

## AI vs. SaaS: Current Signal

**Mechanism:** AI agents perform tasks previously requiring humans with SaaS seats; per-seat pricing collapses when agents replace workers
- *"AI agents compress seat counts by 90%"* (MindStudio)
- Bloomberg projects subscription pricing: 60% → 30% of software pricing; outcome-based: 10% → 60%, over next decade
  - https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2026/saas-ai-agents.html
  - https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/saas-pricing-ai-agent-era

**Market signal:** Public SaaS EV/Revenue fell from 18–19x pandemic peak to 5.1x (March 2026); >$1T market cap erased

**Key structural difference from prior disruptions:** SaaS incumbents are software companies — can embed AI and become the AI product. Microsoft/SAP/Oracle survived cloud transition via same playbook.
- https://www.bain.com/insights/will-agentic-ai-disrupt-saas-technology-report-2025/
- https://insights.flagshipadvisorypartners.com/will-ai-kill-the-b2b-software-industry

**Most exposed:** Rules-based workflow tools (project management, simple CRM). **Most protected:** ERP/financial/compliance systems with deterministic/regulatory moats.
