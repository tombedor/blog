# Research Brief: "Models can't be creative" (creativity.md)

Research for post examining whether AI can be creative and whether there's a market for AI-generated content.

---

## Key Claims to Investigate

1. AI creative tools get a burst of attention then fade — is this pattern real?
2. AI-generated content floods platforms (music, video) but isn't being *consumed* by choice
3. The economics of AI creativity don't work for producers or platforms

---

## 1. Sora (OpenAI Video Generation) — Launch, Peak, Shutdown

**Timeline:**
- Feb 15, 2024: OpenAI first publicly previewed Sora (demo clips only, no public access)
- Dec 9, 2024: Public launch for ChatGPT Pro and Plus users in US/Canada
- March 24, 2026: OpenAI announces shutdown
- April 26, 2026: Sora web/app discontinued
- September 24, 2026: Sora API discontinued

**Usage numbers:**
- Peak daily users: ~1 million (shortly after launch)
- Stabilized at: ~750,000 daily users
- Peak monthly downloads: 3.33 million (November 2025)
- Downloads fell 45% in January 2026
- Downloads fell 66% by February 2026 to 1.13 million

**Economics that killed it:**
- Estimated inference cost: ~$15 million/day
- Total lifetime in-app revenue: $2.1 million (gap was not closable)
- OpenAI chose to reallocate compute to coding/reasoning/text tasks (more profitable)
- The $1 billion Disney licensing/investment deal collapsed — no money ever changed hands
- OpenAI now views Sora's underlying tech as more valuable for robotics than media generation

**Why this matters for the post:** Classic bust pattern — 1M daily users at peak → 750K stabilized → 66% download drop within months → shutdown. The app existed for ~6 months before shutdown announcement. Huge gap between interest and revenue.

## 2. Studio Ghibli AI Image Trend — The Fad Pattern

**Timeline:**
- March 25, 2025: OpenAI enables GPT-4o native image generation for ChatGPT users
- Within days: Viral trend of converting photos to Ghibli-style art explodes
- The trend was especially viral in India

**Peak metrics:**
- ChatGPT weekly active users passed 150 million for the first time ever
- OpenAI added 1 million new users in a single hour (Sam Altman via X)
- ChatGPT app: weekly downloads +11%, active users +5%, in-app purchase revenue +6%
- One Seattle engineer's Ghibli-style image: 46 million views
- Sam Altman: "our GPUs are melting" — OpenAI introduced rate limits for "biblical demand"
- Free tier capped at 3 image generations/day due to load

**Fading:**
- No hard data on when it peaked and dropped; articles from April–May 2025 still discuss it as ongoing
- The trend followed the classic "viral moment" pattern — weeks of saturation, then the algorithm moves on
- No evidence of sustained daily engagement comparable to the peak

**Context/complications:**
- Hayao Miyazaki (Studio Ghibli co-founder, still alive) has called AI "an insult to life itself" (2016 documentary)
- OpenAI already had a refusal system for images in living artists' styles but loosened it
- Copyright concerns widely discussed but no legal action taken (as of research date)

**Why this matters for the post:** The Ghibli trend is a perfect example of novelty driving massive engagement — then evaporating. It was about the *capability reveal* (look what ChatGPT can do now!), not about people having an ongoing need for Ghibli-style images.

## 3. AI Music on Spotify — Flooding the Platform, Not Being Consumed

### The "Perfect Fit Content" Scandal

**Background:** In December 2024/January 2025, journalist Liz Pelly published "The Ghosts in the Machine" in Harper's Magazine (and later a full book, *Mood Machine*, released January 7, 2025). She revealed Spotify's internal "Perfect Fit Content" (PFC) initiative.

**What PFC is:** An internal Spotify program to commission cheap background/ambient tracks from third-party suppliers and quietly place them on curated playlists — optimizing royalty costs. The motivation: tracks from PFC suppliers cost less in royalties than real artists.

**Scale:** By 2023, several hundred playlists were monitored by the PFC team, including popular ambient, jazz, classical, and lo-fi hip-hop playlists. PFC suppliers included Epidemic Sound, Firefly Entertainment, Hush Hush LLC, Catfarm Music AB, Industria Works, and others.

**Earlier history:** A 2022 Swedish newspaper investigation found ~20 musicians producing tracks under 500+ fabricated artist names; 830 fake artists identified, 495 placed on Spotify's curated playlists.

### The AI Spam Flood

**Scale of removals:** In September 2025, Spotify announced it had removed more than 75 million "spammy tracks" over the prior year — representing almost half of Spotify's total archive at the time.

**How it works:** AI tools make it trivial to generate thousands of tracks. The exploit: any stream over 30 seconds generates a royalty payment. Spammers upload huge volumes, target passive-listening playlists, and siphon royalties.

**Tactics targeted:** mass uploads, duplicate tracks with altered metadata, SEO manipulation, artificially short tracks (~31 seconds).

**Spotify's response (September 2025):**
1. New music spam filter
2. Impersonation policy (bans AI voice clones/deepfakes of real artists)
3. Support for DDEX industry standard for AI disclosure in music credits

**Separate story: AI learning content views on Douyin increased 200% YoY** — people watching AI *explain* itself, not consuming AI-generated creative content.

**Why this matters for the post:** The music case is the clearest evidence that AI creative output doesn't have intrinsic demand — it exploits *passive listening* and royalty-payment mechanics. Nobody is seeking out AI music; it's gaming the system.

## 5. General "Burst Then Fade" Pattern for AI Creative Tools

**What the data actually shows — more nuanced than a simple fad:**

The clearest burst-and-fade case is **Sora** (see above). For image generation, the pattern is more complex:

**Image generation volume (through 2024):**
- Stable Diffusion: 12.59 billion images generated total, accounting for 80% of all AI images worldwide; 2 million images/day via official channels; 34 million AI images generated daily across all platforms
- Midjourney: 964 million images generated; 15 million registered users, 1.5–2.5 million active members at any time; 20–40 jobs/second on Discord
- DALL-E: 916 million images generated
- Market share: Midjourney 26.8%, DALL-E 24.35%

**Market size:** Global AI image generator market reached $8.7 billion in 2024, projected to grow to $60.8 billion by 2030 (38.2% CAGR).

**Midjourney specifically (not declining):** Revenue grew from ~$200M (2023) to ~$300M (2024), a 50% increase. No layoffs. Workforce grew from 11 (2022) to 107–163 employees (2025).

**The real pattern:** Rather than "burst then fade," AI image generation has split into:
1. Consumer novelty tools — burst and fade (DALL-E-in-ChatGPT, Ghibli moments)
2. Professional workflow tools — sustained adoption (Midjourney for design, Stable Diffusion for enterprise)

**Video generation (Poe platform data, Jan–May 2025):** Kling video models (from Kuaishou) captured ~30% usage share within weeks of release — classic novelty burst pattern visible in real-time.

**YouTube "AI slop" crackdown (July 2025):**
- 1 in 5 Shorts recommended to new users was low-quality mass-produced AI content
- A Kapwing study found 278 channels producing only AI slop with: 63 billion views, 221 million subscribers, ~$117 million estimated annual ad revenue
- YouTube changed monetization policy July 15, 2025: content must be "significantly original and authentic"
- YouTube CEO Neal Mohan used the term "AI slop" in his January 2026 annual letter

## 6. Are People Actually Consuming AI-Generated Content?

### Consumer Attitudes — Survey Data

**Variety/exclusive survey:**
- 70% of global consumers would be uncomfortable consuming *fully* AI-generated creative content
- 54% uncomfortable with AI-*assisted* content
- Only 37% would be happy to consume AI content if it were the same quality as human-made (down from 44% the prior year — declining acceptance)
- Format matters: ~60% accept AI TV commercials; only 25% accept a 30-minute AI TV show; only 23% accept a full-length AI film
- 3 in 4 US consumers want to know if content was AI-created

**Baringa (US consumer study):** Shifting preferences but persistent skepticism about fully AI-generated creative content.

**Artificial Analysis Generative Media Survey 2025:**
- ~51% neutral or excited about a fully AI-generated film (script, acting, music) willing to pay same or more
- This is a more optimistic framing than the Variety survey

**Video-specific:**
- Nearly 83% of consumers report having watched a video they *suspected* was AI-generated
- Main red flags: robotic gestures (67%), unnatural voices (55%), lack of emotional tone (51%)
- Over a third trust AI-generated video as much as human-made

**AI avatars vs. humans (platform performance data):**
- AI avatars: 10.6% higher watch time on TikTok (short-form)
- AI avatars: 78% completion rate on scalable tutorials
- Human faces: 47% higher subscriber conversion; 3.2x more emotional engagement

### Consumption in Practice

**Short-form AI content:** The 63 billion views on AI slop channels (Kapwing study) shows *passive* consumption — people watching AI content without knowing or caring it's AI, embedded in algorithmic feeds.

**Active/intentional consumption at scale:** Not well-documented. The data suggests most AI creative content is consumed passively (algorithmic placement) rather than sought out.

**AI music:** People listening to AI-generated ambient tracks on Spotify aren't choosing AI music — they're choosing "focus music" or "sleep sounds" and getting AI tracks inserted into those playlists.

**Key tension for the post:** There's a difference between *tolerating* AI content when it's embedded in a feed and *seeking out* AI creative work. The data strongly suggests consumption is primarily the former.

## Counterarguments / Things That Cut Against the Post's Thesis

- **Midjourney is genuinely successful** — $300M revenue in 2024, profitable, growing. Professional image generation has real sustained demand.
- **AI music volume is growing** — AI-generated music tracks on streaming platforms grew 500%+ since 2022 (even before the Spotify crackdown). There may be a genuine market for cheap background music.
- **Short-form AI content does get watched** — 63B views on AI slop channels shows passive consumption at scale. "Nobody wants it" is too strong.
- **Personalization data** — Over 55% of consumers prefer personalized AI-generated videos over generic videos. If AI can be personalized, preferences shift.
- **Time spent with AI platforms is growing** — Consumers' time with AI content projected to grow 29% by 2029 (one survey).

## Potential Angles / Arguments for the Post

1. **The Ghibli moment as archetypal:** People were excited by the *demonstration of capability*, not by the output itself. Once you've seen one Ghibli-style selfie, you've seen them all. The viral moment was about what AI *can* do, not about what people wanted to make or consume repeatedly.

2. **Sora's economics as the tell:** $15M/day to run, $2.1M lifetime revenue. This is what happens when there's no real market — the cost of production is enormous and nobody's paying for the output.

3. **The "AI slop" problem:** AI-generated content floods platforms and is passively consumed, but platforms are actively fighting it (YouTube demonetization, Spotify's 75M track deletion). The content isn't *valued* — it's exploiting algorithmic distribution.

4. **AI music reveals the real market:** The demand for AI music is for *invisible* background content — ambient, lo-fi, focus music. This is music that nobody cares about, almost by definition. It's not a creative market; it's a commodity market.

5. **The survey data on comfort:** Acceptance of fully AI-generated creative content *declined* year-over-year (44% → 37%). As AI gets better, people want it less.
