## Announcement Background

[Project Glasswing](https://www.anthropic.com/project/glasswing) | [Red-team writeup](https://red.anthropic.com/2026/mythos-preview/) | [System card](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-mythos-preview-system-card) | [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/08/anthropic-mythos-model-ai-cyberattack-warning) | [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/10/powell-bessent-us-bank-ceos-anthropic-mythos-ai-cyber.html) | [media notes](media-coverage.md) | [announcement notes](anthropic-announcement.md)

- **April 7, 2026:** Anthropic announced **Claude Mythos Preview** plus **Project Glasswing**, a gated release for critical-infrastructure defenders, launch partners, and 40+ additional organizations.
- Launch partners: AWS, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorganChase, Linux Foundation, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Palo Alto Networks.
- Anthropic committed **$100M usage credits** and **$4M donations** to open-source security orgs; participant pricing is **$25/M input tokens, $125/M output tokens** via Claude API, Bedrock, Vertex AI, and Microsoft Foundry.
- Anthropic claims Mythos found **thousands of zero-days**, but says **over 99%** are unpatched, so the strongest claims are not yet independently auditable.
- Axios amplified the risk framing: officials reportedly believed Mythos could "bring down a Fortune 100 company"; about 40 organizations had access.
- CNBC confirmed that Fed chair Jerome Powell and Treasury secretary Scott Bessent met with major U.S. bank CEOs to discuss Mythos cyber risk; Bloomberg and FT first reported the meeting.

## Claim Checks

[red-team notes](anthropic-red-team.md) | [system-card notes](system-card-notes.md)

- **"Anthropic's Mythos warnings do not seem credible"** — **Too broad.** Public evidence includes real eval deltas: CyberGym **0.83** vs Opus 4.6 **0.67** / Sonnet 4.6 **0.65**; Firefox 147 exploit task **181** working Mythos exploits + **29** register-control cases vs Opus 4.6's **2** working exploits in several hundred attempts; internal OSS-Fuzz-style eval **10 tier-5** full control-flow hijacks. Better critique: "serious claims, but most important evidence is still not externally checkable."
- **"Great press for Mythos capabilities"** — **Strongly supported.** Anthropic calls Mythos its most capable coding/agentic model and surrounds the release with major partner quotes; the warning doubles as enterprise capability marketing.
- **"Jerome Powell meeting is wonderful optics"** — **Supported by media reporting.** CNBC confirmed Powell and Bessent met with major U.S. bank CEOs after Anthropic briefed senior U.S. officials on Mythos's "offensive and defensive cyber applications"; CNBC says Bloomberg and FT first reported the meeting. Bloomberg-reported attendees included BofA, Citigroup, Goldman, Morgan Stanley, and Wells Fargo CEOs; Jamie Dimon was reportedly invited but unable to attend. Pull quote candidates: CNBC video title, "urgent meeting"; CNBC, "ongoing discussions"; Guardian, "unprecedented cybersecurity risks."
- **"They have made this claim before"** — **Supported as historical analogy, not proof of bad faith.** GPT-2 is the cleanest precedent: OpenAI withheld the full model in 2019 because of misuse concerns, released smaller versions first, and used partner research to guide full release ([initial post](https://openai.com/index/better-language-models/), [follow-up](https://openai.com/index/gpt-2-6-month-follow-up/)). Dario and Daniela Amodei are listed on the original GPT-2 post, but that does not prove personal responsibility for the policy. Related but less exact precedents:
  - **GPT-4, March 2023:** OpenAI's system card warned of illicit advice, dual-use capabilities, and risky emergent behaviors, after months of pre-deployment red teaming; the system card also said ARC found GPT-4 was probably not yet capable of autonomous replication/resource acquisition ([system card](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/gpt-4-system-card.pdf)).
  - **OpenAI o1, September/December 2024:** OpenAI classified o1 / o1-preview as **medium risk** for CBRN and persuasion, with low risk for model autonomy and cybersecurity; it also disclosed Apollo scheming evaluations ([system card](https://openai.com/index/openai-o1-system-card/)).
  - **Claude Opus 4, May 2025:** Anthropic released Opus 4 under **ASL-3** as a precaution because it could not rule out CBRN thresholds; TIME reported Anthropic's chief scientist saying the model might help a novice attempt more destructive bioengineering, while emphasizing uncertainty ([Claude 4 system card](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/6c940a1b69ed6a1c/original/Claude-4-System-Card.pdf), [TIME](https://time.com/7287806/anthropic-claude-4-opus-safety-bio-risk/)).
  - **Claude Sonnet 4.5, September 2025:** Anthropic released it under **ASL-3 protections** with CBRN classifiers, though the launch framed it as the company's most aligned frontier model rather than as a withheld-danger model ([announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-4-5)).
- **"'Exploits' are not really exploits / better characterized as bugs"** — **Partly supported for the zero-day-finding section; not as a blanket claim.** Anthropic's announcement says Mythos identified thousands of zero-days, but public details often describe bugs / potential vulnerabilities rather than independently checkable real-world exploit chains. One named FFmpeg finding is explicitly described as a heap out-of-bounds write that Anthropic believed would be challenging to turn into a functioning exploit. However, Anthropic also separately claims working PoC exploits in other contexts: FreeBSD NFS unauthenticated root RCE, Firefox shell exploits, Linux kernel N-day privilege-escalation exploit generation, browser chains, and cyber-range attacks. Strongest post framing: "many headline zero-day findings are better understood as bugs or potential vulnerabilities; Anthropic then uses separate constrained exploit demos to argue the model can sometimes weaponize them."
- **"They have not demonstrated the security-attack capability gap against other labs' models"** — **Substantiated.** The public comparisons mostly show Mythos vs older Anthropic models: CyberGym **0.83** vs Opus 4.6 **0.67** / Sonnet 4.6 **0.65**, and Firefox 147 exploitation vs Opus/Sonnet 4.6. The system card says Mythos was the first model to complete one private cyber range and that no other frontier model had done so, but it does not name the comparator models or publish enough detail to interpret the cross-lab delta. Strong framing: "Anthropic demonstrates a leap over its prior Claude models; it does not publicly establish the same-size gap over GPT/Gemini-class competitors."
- **"The proposed remedy is odd / access is still broad"** — **Supported with nuance.** Anthropic is not making Mythos generally available, but it is distributing through multiple clouds and many vetted organizations. Mitigation is partner vetting + monitoring probes; for this limited preview, Anthropic says it is **not blocking** solely on classifier triggers so defenders can work.
- **"Model leads are not sustainable"** — **Supported for open-source catch-up; mixed for frontier-vs-frontier.** Open-source evidence remains strong in the existing brief. Frontier coding leads: GPT-5's SWE-bench Verified lead lasted about **1.7 months** before Claude Sonnet 4.5; Sonnet 4.5's lead lasted about **1.8 months** before Claude Opus 4.5. But Opus 4.5's same-benchmark lead appears **ongoing** as of April 10, 2026. See [frontier lead notes](../open-source-models/frontier-leads.md).

## Counterarguments / Framing Notes

- Cyber dual-use is real: the same model behavior that finds and patches bugs can speed exploit development.
- Lack of public detail is expected under vulnerability disclosure, but it also weakens the persuasive force of the most alarming claims.
- The self-interest argument is plausible: restricted access can favor incumbents and support regulation against less-controlled distribution. Treat that as an incentives critique, not a direct refutation of the capability evidence.
