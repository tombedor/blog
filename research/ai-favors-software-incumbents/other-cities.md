# Source Note: Other City Taxi / Ride-Hail Comparisons

## Bottom line

- **Portland is the best secondary case study found.** Before its 2015 policy change, Portland had relatively few taxis for its population and regulations made fleet expansion difficult. After Uber and Lyft entered, total daily rides rose quickly while taxi volume initially appeared steady.
- Portland does **not** cleanly prove that ride-hail first won in taxi-underserved neighborhoods. The city's early analysis found TNC rides concentrated downtown and taxi rides more dispersed, though TNC use grew in East Portland and waits were shorter than taxis on average.
- **Chicago has the best public raw data** for a future neighborhood-level comparison: taxi trips from 2013 onward and TNP trips from November 2018 onward, both with community-area geography. It cannot show Uber's initial market entry because TNP reporting begins years later.
- **San Francisco supports broad geographic availability, not a clean underserved-city story.** In 2016, 33% of TNC trips served Communities of Concern and estimated waits were roughly equal inside and outside those communities, but San Francisco was already a taxi-heavy market.

## Portland

- Portland removed taxi permit limits and admitted TNCs in 2015. The city auditor reported that the old system had made taxi fleet expansion and new entry difficult.
- The auditor said Portland had relatively few taxis for its population before deregulation.
- City data showed daily rides rising from roughly 7,000 to 10,000 during May-August 2015. By April 2016, TNC rides outnumbered roughly 3,000 daily taxi rides; taxi rides appeared steady.
- Early TNC rides were concentrated in the city center, while taxi rides were more dispersed. TNC ridership nevertheless grew in East Portland.
- Average reported waits were six minutes for TNCs and eight minutes for taxis, with longer waits farther from downtown.
- https://www.portland.gov/sites/default/files/2021/488-tnc-taxis-published.pdf

## Chicago

- Chicago explicitly regulated "Neighborhood Cabs" and underserved-area service before TNCs, evidence that geographic taxi undersupply was a recognized policy problem.
- Public trip-level datasets support pickup comparisons by community area:
  - Taxi trips, 2013-2023: https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Taxi-Trips-2013-2023-/wrvz-psew
  - Taxi trips, 2024 onward: https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Taxi-Trips-2024-/ajtu-isnz
  - TNP trips, Nov. 2018-2022: https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Transportation-Network-Providers-Trips-2018-2022-/m6dm-c72p
  - TNP trips, 2023-2024: https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Transportation-Network-Providers-Trips-2023-2024-/n26f-ihde
  - TNP trips, 2025 onward: https://data.cityofchicago.org/Transportation/Transportation-Network-Providers-Trips-2025-/6dvr-xwnh
- Best future chart: taxi versus TNP pickups per 1,000 residents, split between the Loop/airport areas and historically underserved community areas. This would test geographic expansion, but only from late 2018 onward.
- https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/dol/rulesandregs/PublicChauffeurRules.pdf

## San Francisco

- SFCTA estimated roughly equal three-minute ride-hail access times inside and outside Communities of Concern.
- About 33% of trips served Communities of Concern; service was available on weekends, overnight, and in southern neighborhoods.
- This is useful evidence that an app-dispatched network can provide broad coverage, but San Francisco is less useful than Portland for showing latent demand created by scarce taxi supply.
- https://www.sfcta.org/sites/default/files/2019-02/Emerging%20Mobility%20Studies_11.pdf
