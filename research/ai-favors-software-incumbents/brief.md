# Research Brief: Internet Disruption Analogies for AI vs. Software Incumbents

**Scope:** Compare pre-internet incumbents with internet/software disruptors, then test whether the analogy carries over to AI attacking software incumbents.

**Bottom line:**
- Travel agents support an interface-shift argument, but not a simple analog-to-digital argument: agencies already used computerized reservation systems, and large agencies adapted better than small agencies.
- Classifieds and taxis show different incumbent moats: bundled distribution and regulatory scarcity.
- Technology transitions sort incumbents rather than uniformly replacing them. Adaptable technology, data, customer relationships, and scale helped large travel agencies survive the internet transition.
- AI disruption is structurally different because software incumbents already own more of the workflow/data layer that AI needs, although that advantage does not guarantee they retain the customer interface.

## Travel Agents

**Claim:** The internet shifted routine travel search and booking from an agent-operated digital system to customer self-service.

- Online airline bookings rose **7% -> 30% from 1999 to 2002**; small-agency air sales fell **32% from 1995 to 2002**.
- This was not uniform incumbent failure: very large agency sales nearly doubled as corporate travel, scale, and service fees retained value.
- Corporate customers later abandoned routine agent-assisted booking, not managed travel itself. Amex GBT processed **$36.3B** in 2025 travel with **96%** client retention, but **83%** of its bookings were digital.
- No defensible pre-internet-to-present revenue series exists: pre-2014 travel revenue was mixed into American Express's broader Travel Related Services segment, and the later GBT reporting perimeter changed through the 2014 joint venture and acquisitions.
- Gross booking volume does show survival at scale. In 2025 dollars, American Express reported **$31.9B** of travel sales in 1995; Legacy GBT generated **$34.9B** of TTV in 2019; Amex GBT generated **$36.3B** in 2025. The 1995 figure includes consumer travel, while later figures are business travel, so this is directional rather than a like-for-like growth series.
- Traditional agents already used CRS/GDS software. The defensible distinction is customer interface, channel economics, scale, and complementary assets, not "phones/fax versus computers."
- AI more directly complements assets held by software incumbents: ICT skills, digital workflows, proprietary data, infrastructure, and integrations. Those assets improve the odds of adaptation but do not eliminate organizational inertia or AI-native interface risk.

## Classified Ads / Newspapers

### Background

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Newspapers owned the local buyer/seller marketplace because classified discovery was bundled into print distribution. Scarcity worked in their favor: ad placement was paid, local, slow, and hard to search, but buyers already looked in the paper.

**Classified revenue chronology:**
- Classified ad revenue peaked in 2000 at **$19.6B**.
- By 2012, classified revenue was **$4.6B**, a 77% drop in 12 years.
- Craigslist, founded in 1995, is estimated to have cost newspapers **$5B** in classified revenue from 2000-2007.
- https://phys.org/news/2013-08-craigslist-newspapers-billions.html
- https://www.minnpost.com/business/2014/02/how-craigslist-killed-newspapers-golden-goose/

### Claims

**Claim: Newspaper classifieds were vulnerable because classifieds were a high-margin transaction layer bundled inside a news product.**

Supporting evidence:
- Classifieds were ~40% of newspaper ad revenue and ~30% of total revenue around 2000.
- Classifieds carried high margins and subsidized newsroom economics.
- https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/142129/2000-2010-the-decade-that-killed-newspaper-clas.html
- https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/11138/ad-spending-in-newspapers-hit-487-billion-in-200.html
- Pull quote: "Classifieds used to be a mainstay of the newspaper business" (MediaPost)

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- Newspaper decline was not only Craigslist: search ads, social media, changing reader behavior, print cost structure, and the 2008 recession all contributed.
- The strongest causal studies try to isolate classified competition from other industry shocks.

**Claim: Craigslist did not need better journalism; it won by unbundling the transaction layer.**

Supporting evidence:
- Craigslist made posting self-serve, searchable, low-cost/free, and no longer dependent on print distribution.
- Peer-reviewed source: "Impact of Online Competition on Local Newspapers: Evidence from the Introduction of Craigslist," *Review of Economic Studies* (2024).
- https://academic.oup.com/restud/article/92/3/1738/7665573
- https://cepr.org/publications/dp16130
- https://web.stanford.edu/~gjmartin/papers/Craigslist_Final.pdf
- Pull quote: "separate the effect of competition for classified advertising from other changes" (CEPR)

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- Craigslist was not a full newspaper replacement; it attacked one profitable revenue stream.
- The analogy is strongest for bundled businesses with separable transaction layers, weaker for products where content/workflow and monetization are tightly integrated.

## Cab Drivers / Taxi Medallions

### Background

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Taxi incumbency was legal and operational, not informational. Medallions created a capped right to street hails; dispatch radio networks handled pre-arranged rides; consumers had poor visibility into supply, wait time, price, driver quality, or route quality.
- https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/economic-analysis-taxicab-regulation/233832.pdf
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/taxis/

**Why medallions were introduced:** The Haas Act (1937) was a Depression-era response to taxi oversupply, weak driver earnings, congestion, and public disorder/service problems. NYC's Board of Aldermen capped licenses at 13,595 after finding that too many taxis produced depressed earnings and congested streets; as some owners stopped renewing, active medallions fell to 11,787 and stayed there until the first city auction in 1996.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2020/01/Taxi-Medallion-Task-Force-Report-Final.pdf
- https://www.schallerconsult.com/taxi/taxi2.htm
- https://hrlr.law.columbia.edu/hrlr-online/distressed-drivers-solving-the-new-york-city-taxi-medallion-debt-crisis/
- Pull quotes:
  - "depressed driver earnings and congested city streets" (NYC Taxi Medallion Task Force)
  - "violent protest and the demands for industry regulation increased" (Columbia Human Rights Law Review)

**Medallion collapse signal:**
- NYC had 13,587 medallions in 2019.
- NYC medallions averaged **$1.16M** in March 2014 for wheelchair-accessible corporate medallions; by Nov. 2019 average all-medallion price was **$164,518**.
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/taxis/
- Pull quote: "the medallion secondary market was essentially 'frozen'" (NYC Taxi Medallion Task Force)

### Claims

**Claim: The medallion moat began as public-order regulation, then drifted into regulatory scarcity.**

Supporting evidence:
- The original policy goal was stabilization/order: reduce oversupply, improve driver income, reduce congestion, and manage disorder.
- Transferability plus a long-running cap converted licenses into appreciating assets; the Task Force says the cap and transferability "created a valuable asset."
- https://council.nyc.gov/data/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2020/01/Taxi-Medallion-Task-Force-Report-Final.pdf
- https://www.schallerconsult.com/taxi/taxi2.htm
- Pull quote: "created a valuable asset by establishing a virtual monopoly" (NYC Taxi Medallion Task Force)

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- The cap also served real regulatory goals: safety, service quality, congestion, and driver-income stability.
- Medallion values were shaped by lending practices, city auctions, and financial speculation, not just the license cap itself.

**Claim: Uber bypassed a fragile scarcity model by making dispatch, trust, and payment software-native.**

Supporting evidence:
- Uber's advantage was mobile software more than generic "internet": GPS, maps, app dispatch, stored payments, two-sided ratings, and price/supply matching.
- Non-medallion drivers could compete with taxis on convenience even without the street-hail right.
- Per-capita NYC trip data through April 2026 shows Uber overtaking yellow taxis while combined ride volume expanded.
- The durable product was a better interface to the existing ride market.
- https://toddwschneider.com/dashboards/nyc-taxi-ridehailing-uber-lyft-data/
- https://time.com/3609114/taxi-medallion-prices-decline-uber-uber-lyft/
- https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2015/11/cab-medallion-owners-sue-nyc-blame-uber-for-ruining-business/
- [Other-city comparison](other-cities.md)

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- Do not over-attribute the medallion crash to Uber alone. NYC's Task Force and later reporting point to inflated prices, aggressive lending, and city revenue incentives.
- Better framing: software exposed and bypassed a fragile regulatory scarcity model.
- https://hrlr.law.columbia.edu/hrlr-online/distressed-drivers-solving-the-new-york-city-taxi-medallion-debt-crisis/

## Encyclopedia Britannica

### Background

**Pre-internet incumbent position:** Curated authority at premium price (~$1,400-$2,000/set); door-to-door sales force.

**Collapse chronology:** Peak 1990: >100,000 sets/year; print discontinued in 2012; by then print was 1% of revenue.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica
- https://hbr.org/2013/03/encyclopaedia-britannicas-president-on-killing-off-a-244-year-old-product

### Claims

**Claim: Britannica was not mainly a feature battle; it was an economics battle.**

Supporting evidence:
- Microsoft Encarta undercut on price first; Wikipedia made curation free and continuously updated.

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- Britannica survived as a digital education/reference company; the print product died, not the institution.

## AI vs. Software Incumbents

### Background

**Current market signal:** Public SaaS EV/Revenue fell from 18-19x pandemic peak to 5.1x (March 2026); IGV software ETF down ~30% from Sep. 2025 peak; ~$2T in market cap erased.
- https://www.saastr.com/the-saas-rout-of-2026-is-even-worse-than-you-think-for-the-first-time-ever-software-now-trades-at-a-discount-to-the-sp-500/

**SaaSpocalypse company scorecard** — see `saaspocalypse-companies.md` for full price history:

| Company | Peak | May 2026 | Delta | Threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Asana (ASAN) | $145 (Nov. 2021) | ~$7 | -95% | High: task tracking assumes human workers |
| Monday.com (MNDY) | ~$450 (Nov. 2021) | ~$84 | ~-81% | High: same |
| HubSpot (HUBS) | $852 (Nov. 2021) | $221 | -74% | High: horizontal CRM/marketing |
| Workday (WDAY) | $307 (Feb. 2024) | $128 | -58% | Medium: HR/finance has compliance moat |

| ServiceNow (NOW) | n/a | down ~33% from 2025 high | mixed | Adapting: outcome-based pricing, 21% revenue growth |

### Claims

**Claim: AI threatens seat-based SaaS by attacking the human labor assumed by the product/pricing model.**

Supporting evidence:
- AI agents can perform tasks previously requiring humans with SaaS seats; per-seat pricing weakens when agents replace workers.
- Bloomberg projects subscription pricing shrinking from 60% -> 30% of software pricing, while outcome-based pricing rises from 10% -> 60% over the next decade.
- https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2026/saas-ai-agents.html
- https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/saas-pricing-ai-agent-era

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- Some SaaS products are systems of record, compliance layers, or infrastructure rather than labor proxies.
- Revenue pressure on public SaaS also reflects interest rates, pandemic multiple compression, and broader market repricing.

**Claim: AI disruption is structurally different from internet disruption because SaaS incumbents are already software companies.**

Supporting evidence:
- SaaS incumbents can embed AI into existing workflows and data surfaces rather than abandon their operating model.
- Microsoft/SAP/Oracle survived cloud transition via similar adaptation; ServiceNow is a plausible adapting-incumbent analog.
- 2025 bifurcation: Palantir +142%, Cloudflare +80%, MongoDB +70% vs. HubSpot -51%, Monday.com -36%, Salesforce -31%; infrastructure and AI-native won, horizontal seat-based software lost.
- https://www.bain.com/insights/will-agentic-ai-disrupt-saas-technology-report-2025/
- https://insights.flagshipadvisorypartners.com/will-ai-kill-the-b2b-software-industry
- https://www.saastr.com/the-performing-major-b2b-stocks-of-2025-what-the-ai-divide-tells-us-about-the-future-of-saas/

Contradicting / complicating evidence:
- Owning the workflow does not guarantee owning the new interface; AI-native entrants can sit above incumbent systems.
- Horizontal workflow tools with weak data moats may be more like classifieds than like Microsoft/SAP/Oracle.
- Most exposed: horizontal workflow tools (task tracking, generic CRM, marketing automation). Most protected: ERP/financial/compliance systems and infrastructure (Stripe, Twilio, Snowflake).
