# Source Note: Technology Timeline and the Recurring Spam Pattern

## Working Definition

Use **political spam** narrowly for unsolicited, substantially similar political messages sent in bulk. Use **the spam logic** for earlier media: reducing the sender’s marginal cost and shifting attention, sorting, and avoidance costs to recipients or intermediaries.

This avoids claiming that an eighteenth-century pamphlet was literally spam while preserving the historical through-line.

## 1. Print, Pamphlets, and the Partisan Press

- Revolutionary politics spread through pamphlets, posters, newspapers, and broadsides. The Library of Congress notes that 29 newspapers printed the Declaration and colonial presses produced 19 additional broadside editions.
  - https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/bay-psalm-book-and-american-printing/online-exhibition.html
- *Common Sense* was first printed January 10, 1776 and became the exemplary high-reach persuasive pamphlet.
  - https://blogs.loc.gov/headlinesandheroes/2026/01/250-years-ago-thomas-paines-common-sense/
- Newspapers were partisan institutions. LOC reports that partisan politics helped grow the number of newspapers from 92 in 1790 to 329 by the end of Jefferson’s presidency.
  - https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/jefferson/jefffed.html
- The 1792 postal law set very low newspaper rates and let printers exchange papers free, explicitly treating circulating political information as a public good.
  - https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/periodicals-postage-history.htm

**Change in affordance:** one speaker could be copied, reprinted, and carried far beyond a meeting place. Gatekeeping sat with printers, editors, patrons, and the postal system.

**Do not overstate:** circulation was costly, finite, and often subscriber- or public-space based. It lacked the private-channel intrusion and near-zero marginal cost associated with modern spam.

## 2. Telegraph, Mass Newspapers, and Mixed Media

- The telegraph accelerated news and nationalized the information cycle, but it usually fed newspapers rather than delivering campaign messages into individual homes.
- Charles Musser’s *Politicking and Emergent Media* documents a surprisingly mixed 1890s campaign environment: illustrated lectures, phonograph, cinema, telegraph, and telephone.
  - https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520292734/politicking-and-emergent-media

**Change in affordance:** speed and synchronization. Campaigns could coordinate and react nationally, while mass newspapers widened reach.

**Why this is less “spammy”:** strong intermediaries still rationed access. The key shift was fast mass publicity, not automated person-by-person contact.

## 3. Postal Direct Mail and Computerized Lists

- The National Postal Museum has examples of campaign mail at least as early as an 1864 Lincoln envelope.
  - https://postalmuseum.si.edu/what-is-election-mail-and-its-history-in-the-us
- Direct mail became a presidential technique in the 1950s–1970s. Dennis Johnson identifies Eisenhower, Goldwater, and McGovern and pioneers Richard Viguerie, Morris Dees, and Roger Craver.
  - https://academic.oup.com/book/27768/chapter-abstract/198005508
- Moriyama shows how computerized lists and millions of unsolicited letters created movement infrastructure, not just individual campaign advertising.
  - https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv2n4w5r7

**Change in affordance:** addressable mass communication. A sender could select a segment, measure donations, refine copy, reuse a list, and rent that list to allies.

**Durable legacy:** modern email and SMS fundraising inherit direct mail’s emotional urgency, list economics, performance testing, and perpetual campaign relationship.

## 4. Telephone Banks and Robocalls

- Live phone banks added immediate response and a semblance of personal contact. Computerized voter files made them targetable.
- A 1984 Washington Post account described a national computer-assisted effort to locate/register Republican voters; a 1986 account described a broader telemarketing program that included roughly two million automated calls from President Reagan.
  - https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1984/04/22/high-tech-impersonal-computer-net-is-snaring-prospective-republicans/8e57155c-4224-4661-97d5-25cef8c582b9/
  - https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/1986/10/21/hired-hands-are-busy-dialing-as-gop-calls-for-big-turnout/5db45c13-debb-4fbe-85c2-d11de931d454/
- By the 2008 presidential primaries, 44% of voters in surveyed states reported at least one campaign robocall.
  - https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2008/03/27/section-3-voters-targeted-by-robo-calls/
- CRS warns that robocall activity is unusually hard to reconstruct: public data, campaign disclosures, and scholarly work are limited.
  - https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/PDF/RL34361/RL34361.15.pdf

**Change in affordance:** campaigns could enter the home, demand synchronous attention, capture responses, and later substitute recordings for callers.

## 5. Email

- Grossman identifies 1998 as the first widespread U.S. election use of unsolicited political bulk email.
  - https://btlj.org/data/articles2015/vol19/19_4/19-berkeley-tech-l-j-FM-1157-1575.pdf
- In June 2003, 74% of email users told Pew that unsolicited political or advocacy email was spam.
  - https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2005/03/07/the-politics-of-spam/
- By 2010, 31% of registered voters said they had received political email, compared with 14% in 2006.
  - https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2010/11/11/section-4-campaign-outreach/
- Campaign email became a test-and-optimize system. ProPublica’s 2012 “Message Machine” captured many variants sent by campaigns.
  - https://projects.propublica.org/emails/

**Change in affordance:** marginal distribution cost approached zero; messages became instantaneous, personalized, measurable, and trivially repeatable.

**Governance response:** filters and sender reputation—not only law—became private regulators. That creates a recurring political fight over whether filtering is protection, censorship, or algorithmic bias.

## 6. Social Platforms and Automated Accounts

- Platforms combine public posting, algorithmic distribution, paid targeting, fake identity, and user-to-user forwarding.
- The 2016 Internet Research Agency operation created false U.S. personas, organic communities, paid ads, and events.
  - https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco/file/1035477/dl
  - https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/2019/10/08/press-senate-intel-committee-releases-bipartisan-report-russia-e2-80-99s-use-social-media/
- Research on 14 million Twitter messages found bots particularly active in the early amplification of low-credibility content.
  - https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.07592

**Change in affordance:** the recipient can be made to encounter propaganda through apparent peers rather than a declared campaign. Distribution is partly outsourced to ranking algorithms and real users.

## 7. Peer-to-Peer Political Texting

- By 2018, peer-to-peer texting was a standard campaign consideration. TIME observed about a dozen volunteers send 10,000 messages in under an hour.
  - https://time.com/5432309/politician-campaigns-midterm-election-text-messages/
- Hustle reports sending 475 million texts in 2020, up 137% from 2018.
  - https://www.techforcampaigns.org/impact/2020-texting-analysis
- Industry blocker estimates of total U.S. political texts run into the billions, but should be labeled estimates, not audited totals.

**Change in affordance:** campaigns reached a channel recipients still perceived as personal. Software preserved a formal human initiation step while enabling assembly-line throughput.

**Legal hinge:** after *Facebook v. Duguid*, a system generally is not a TCPA “automatic telephone dialing system” unless it stores or produces numbers using a random or sequential number generator. Campaigns usually work from voter/contact lists.
  - https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-511_p86b.pdf

## 8. AI Voice and Conversational LLMs

- The FCC confirmed in 2024 that AI-generated voices fall within the TCPA’s “artificial or prerecorded voice” category.
  - https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-84A1_Rcd.pdf
- ODNI observed Russia and Iran using generative AI in 2024 influence operations but said it accelerated existing activity rather than revolutionizing it.
  - https://www.dni.gov/files/FMIC/documents/ODNI-Election-Security-Update-20240923.pdf
- OpenAI reported foreign covert operations using models to draft comments/articles, translate, create account biographies, research, and debug code; it did not find meaningful audience expansion from the model use in the cases discussed.
  - https://openai.com/index/disrupting-deceptive-uses-of-ai-by-covert-influence-operations/

**Change in affordance:** a political sender can now maintain many distinct, responsive conversations at once. Unlike a robocall or template text, the message can adapt to the recipient’s objections and disclose information selectively.

**Evidence caution:** public documentation of deployed, unsolicited, one-to-one LLM political persuasion in the U.S. is still sparse. The local “Emma” transcript is evidence of chatbot-like behavior, not technical proof of an LLM.

## Recurring Cycle

1. A medium opens and initially feels scarce, personal, or trustworthy.
2. Political actors import lists and targeting methods from commerce.
3. Cheap volume overwhelms the channel’s old attention norms.
4. Recipients adopt avoidance/filtering; platforms add private rules.
5. Law catches up to a technical definition that the next workflow can route around.
6. The next medium restores novelty and attention—temporarily.
