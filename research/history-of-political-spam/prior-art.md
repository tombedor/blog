# Source Note: Prior Art on Political Spam and Communication Technology

## Start Here

### Seth Grossman, “Keeping Unwanted Donkeys and Elephants Out of Your Inbox” (2004)

- Full text: https://btlj.org/data/articles2015/vol19/19_4/19-berkeley-tech-l-j-FM-1157-1575.pdf
- Closest prior work to the proposed post: defines political spam as unsolicited political bulk email; dates its first widespread U.S. use to the 1998 elections; compares opt-in, opt-out, and labeling systems; and argues that political spam can be covered by a general content-neutral law without violating the First Amendment.
- Particularly reusable observation: the factors driving political spam are the same as commercial spam—low cost, ease of distribution, and a growing reachable population.
- Limitation: stops at email and predates social media, modern peer-to-peer texting, *Facebook v. Duguid*, and generative AI.

### Finn Brunton, *Spam: A Shadow History of the Internet* (MIT Press, 2013)

- Publisher page: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262527576/spam/
- Best conceptual history: spam as an arms race over attention, trust, norms, filtering, and control of a shared communications system.
- Useful periodization: early noncommercial networks (1970s–1995), commercial Internet (1995–2003), then algorithmic spam-versus-filter conflict.
- Limitation: a history of digital spam generally, not a history of U.S. political campaigning.

## Channel Histories

### Early print, postal networks, and party newspapers

- Jeffrey L. Pasley, *The Tyranny of Printers: Newspaper Politics in the Early American Republic*: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt6wrm5f
  - Strongest source for the claim that early newspapers were working institutions of parties rather than outside observers.
- Richard R. John, *Spreading the News: The American Postal System from Franklin to Morse* (1995).
  - Standard history of the postal system as a deliberately constructed national information network.
- USPS, “Postage Rates for Periodicals: A Narrative History”: https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/periodicals-postage-history.htm
  - Primary institutional summary of the 1792 newspaper subsidy and free printer exchanges.
- Library of Congress, “First Among Many”: https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/bay-psalm-book-and-american-printing/online-exhibition.html
  - Strong visual/primary-source survey of pamphlets, posters, newspapers, and broadsides during the Revolution.
- Charles Musser, *Politicking and Emergent Media* (2016): https://www.ucpress.edu/book/9780520292734/politicking-and-emergent-media
  - Valuable corrective to claims of digital novelty: the 1890s already had a mixed political media system of illustrated lectures, phonograph, cinema, telegraph, and telephone.

### Direct mail, consulting, voter files, and phone banks

- Dennis W. Johnson, *Democracy for Hire: A History of American Political Consulting* (2016): https://academic.oup.com/book/27768
- Key chapter, “Voter Contact: Targeting, Direct Mail, and Phone Banks”: https://academic.oup.com/book/27768/chapter-abstract/198005508
  - Best compact chronology: Eisenhower, Goldwater, and McGovern direct mail; Viguerie, Morris Dees, Roger Craver; voter files and lifestyle clusters; telemarketing and robocalls.
- Nadine Kishiye Moriyama, *Empire of Direct Mail* (2022), open access: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv2n4w5r7
  - Best deep treatment of computerized direct mail, list building, fundraising, anger/fear appeals, and conservative movement infrastructure from 1950–1980.
- CRS, “Automated Political Telephone Calls (‘Robo Calls’) in Federal Campaigns”: https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/PDF/RL34361/RL34361.15.pdf
  - Best federal overview of robocall rules and evidence gaps. Important finding: public spending data and scholarship on automated political calls are thin.
- Donald Green and Alan Gerber, *Get Out the Vote!*: https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7864/j.ctt1gpcdbr
  - Evidence on the effectiveness of canvassing, direct mail, phone banks, and robocalls; useful if the post asks whether volume actually persuades or mobilizes.

### Internet, email, and data-driven campaigning

- Kirsten Foot and Steven Schneider, *Web Campaigning* (2006): https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262062589/web-campaigning/
  - Systematic study of campaign websites in 2000, 2002, and 2004.
- Andrew Paul Williams and Kaye D. Trammell, “Candidate Campaign E-Mail Messages in the Presidential Election 2004”: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0002764205279438
- Pew, “The Politics of Spam” (2005): https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2005/03/07/the-politics-of-spam/
  - Best contemporaneous evidence that recipients themselves treated unsolicited political email as spam: 74% did in 2003.
- Pew, “Online Politics in 2008”: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2009/04/15/online-politics-in-2008/
- Diana Owen, “New Media and Political Campaigns,” *Oxford Handbook of Political Communication*: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34621/chapter/294956504
- Jill Lepore, *If Then* (2020).
  - History of the Simulmatics Corporation and 1960-era behavioral prediction; useful prehistory for data-driven voter modeling and the recurring claim that computation will make persuasion scientific.

### Bots, influence operations, and AI

- Samuel Woolley, *Manufacturing Consensus* (2023): https://academic.oup.com/yale-scholarship-online/book/51419
  - Eight years of fieldwork on automated/anonymized propaganda across countries; strongest bridge from campaign technology to covert influence.
- U.S. Senate Intelligence Committee, “Russia’s Use of Social Media” (2019): https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/2019/10/08/press-senate-intel-committee-releases-bipartisan-report-russia-e2-80-99s-use-social-media/
- DOJ Internet Research Agency indictment: https://www.justice.gov/archives/sco/file/1035477/dl
- ODNI Election Security Update, Sept. 2024: https://www.dni.gov/files/FMIC/documents/ODNI-Election-Security-Update-20240923.pdf
  - Best cautious formulation: generative AI improved and accelerated some foreign influence tasks but had not revolutionized operations.
- OpenAI, “Disrupting deceptive uses of AI by covert influence operations” (2024): https://openai.com/index/disrupting-deceptive-uses-of-ai-by-covert-influence-operations/
  - Concrete task-level examples: article and comment generation, translation, fake biography creation, research, and code debugging; no meaningful audience lift detected in the operations discussed.
- Josh A. Goldstein et al., “Forecasting Potential Misuses of Language Models for Disinformation Campaigns” (2023): https://openai.com/index/forecasting-misuse/
  - Framework separates model creation/access from content generation, dissemination, and audience effect. Helpful against the lazy assumption that cheaper content automatically means successful persuasion.

## Synthesis Gap the Post Can Fill

No single source found joins all four of these into one U.S. narrative:

1. the pre-digital history of cheap political reach;
2. the legal divergence between political and commercial solicitation;
3. foreign actors adapting the same channels; and
4. the change from bulk one-way messages to individualized LLM dialogue.

Grossman is the closest exact prior art; Johnson/Moriyama supply the analog campaign history; Brunton supplies the spam theory; Woolley plus government threat reports supply the foreign/automated influence history.
