# Source Note: Political SMS Vendors and the Fundraising Loop

**Status date:** October 19, 2026. Figures are reporting estimates or vendor claims unless identified as official fees. FEC disbursements show what committees paid, not a vendor's profit.

## Bottom Line

- Political SMS is now a mature but fragmented industry. There is no reliable census because “texting company” can mean a software platform, campaign consultant, list broker, outsourced reply shop, carrier-registration service, or fundraising agency.
- The transmission cost is usually the smallest visible layer: roughly **1.5–4 cents per SMS segment at campaign scale**. Donor acquisition, list rental, creative/testing, consulting, and full-service fundraising can cost much more.
- There is no industry-wide answer to how much of an SMS donation reaches political work. A direct donation through ActBlue or WinRed loses about 4% to processing; what the recipient committee later spends on fundraising is a separate question.
- Recent reporting documents particular PAC/vendor networks in which nearly all receipts were recycled into consultants and further fundraising. Those are important cases, not a valid average for every campaign or text.

## The Vendor Stack

| Layer | Function | Illustrative firms/services |
|---|---|---|
| Data and targeting | Voter/mobile matching, modeled affiliation, donor lists, segmentation | Commercial voter-file and list vendors; sometimes bundled by consultants |
| Sending platform | P2P/broadcast SMS, number provisioning, replies, opt-outs, reporting | Hustle, GetThru/ThruText, Scale to Win, RumbleUp, Opn Sesame, FullPAC/RoboCent, CallHub |
| Digital agency | Strategy, copy, creative testing, list acquisition, fundraising optimization; may subcontract transmission | Mothership Strategies, Message Digital, MissionWired, Targeted Victory, Aisle 518, Sapphire Strategies |
| Network/compliance | Campaign Verify, 10DLC registration, carrier routing/filtering | The Campaign Registry, Campaign Verify, carriers and aggregators |
| Contribution processor | Donation form, card processing, allocation and reporting | ActBlue, WinRed |

Names are illustrative, not endorsements or a complete market map. Firms change services and partisan client policies. The distinction matters: the company appearing in an SMS link, the committee receiving the donation, the agency writing the copy, and the platform transmitting the text may be four different entities.

### Evidence of scale and fragmentation

- FullPAC's 2025 SEC offering circular calls campaign services “highly competitive and fragmented,” says more than 5,000 campaigns had used its RoboCent tools, and reports roughly 30 contract agents individually initiating P2P messages. These are company representations in an offering document, not independent market totals.
  - https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2076148/000149315225026771/form253g2.htm
- Targeted Victory currently advertises **12 billion texts delivered** and **$2.3 billion raised** across its work. Again, those are cumulative vendor claims, useful for scale rather than audited market share.
  - https://targetedvictory.com/political/
- Contemporary vendor comparisons routinely list numerous P2P platforms, and several agencies bundle texting with email, data, digital advertising, and fundraising. There is therefore no clean count of “SMS firms.”

## What a $100 Text Donation Can Mean

### Direct candidate/organization page

- ActBlue's standard published fee is **3.95%**; WinRed's grassroots fee is **3.94%**. On a $100 donation, about $96 reaches the named committee before that committee's subsequent expenses.
  - https://www.actblue.com/pricing/
  - https://support.winred.com/en/articles/3097721-pricing
- Neither figure measures the cost of acquiring the donor. The committee may separately pay for lists, texts, agency commissions, salaries, advertising, and repeated solicitations.

### PAC or advocacy-group page

- The processing fee is still only the first deduction. The recipient PAC controls the remaining money and may spend it on candidates, independent expenditures, organizing, issue work, overhead, consultants, or more fundraising.
- A committee's name can imply that a donation will help candidates without promising that most receipts will be transferred directly to candidate committees. The useful test is its FEC disbursement record, not the emotional framing of the text.

### Why gross receipts mislead

Suppose an agency spends $70 acquiring donors and generates $100 in contributions. Gross fundraising is $100; apparent “profit” is not $100, and the committee has only $30 before other expenses. If that $30 buys another acquisition campaign, the operation can produce impressive fundraising totals while contributing little cash to candidates or voter persuasion.

FEC descriptions can compound the ambiguity: a payment to a lead agency may include pass-through carrier, advertising, list, or subcontractor costs. It is unsafe to equate reported vendor payments with profit.

## The Mothership Case

This appears to be the recent story behind the user's question.

- Stanford political scientist Adam Bonica analyzed a network of PACs linked operationally to Democratic digital fundraiser Mothership Strategies. He estimated that the network raised about **$678 million** from individuals since 2018, paid **$159 million** directly to Mothership, paid **$22.5 million** to texting specialist Message Digital, and transferred at most **$11 million** to candidates, campaigns, or national party committees—his **1.6%** “fundraising efficiency” measure.
  - https://data4democracy.substack.com/p/the-mothership-vortex-an-investigation
  - https://www.thenation.com/article/politics/mothership-strategies-democratic-consultants-fundraising-pleas/
- That does **not** mean Mothership booked 98.4% as profit or that every donor was told the money would go directly to a candidate. The denominator is the receipts of a selected PAC network; the numerator counts transfers to candidates/party committees, not every possible PAC program or independent expenditure.
- Mothership disputed the analysis. It told *The Nation* that it had directly raised more than $400 million for campaigns, PACs, and issue groups, and that many payments reported under its name were pass-through funds paid to advertising and other program vendors.
- The defensible conclusion is narrower: **the network shows how a solicitation operation can recycle most receipts through fundraising, consultants, payroll, lists, and vendor costs while transferring little to candidates.** The exact mission value and vendor profit cannot be read directly from the headline ratios.

## The Broader Fundraising-Industrial-Complex Claim

- Bonica's algorithmic classification of FEC disbursements estimates approximately **$3 billion** in 2024 political fundraising costs. His presentation gives roughly one-third as the share consumed by fundraising, compared with nine cents per dollar in 2004; exact percentages vary in his article depending on whether the denominator is receipts or expenditures.
  - https://data4democracy.substack.com/p/the-fundraising-industrial-complex
- He reports donor-list spending rising from about **$20 million in 2004** to **$214 million in 2024**, illustrating that the reusable donor identity can be as valuable as the first donation.
- This is a researcher-built categorization, not an official FEC “SMS cost” series. Bundled vendor payments and vague purpose descriptions make precise channel attribution impossible.
- A separate ProPublica investigation found a network of political nonprofits that raised more than **$33 million** and spent over **90%** on fundraising. That investigation demonstrates the longstanding business model but centered heavily on telephone fundraising and should not be presented as an SMS-industry statistic.
  - https://www.propublica.org/article/political-nonprofits-fundraising-ftc-irs-527s-pacs

## Why SMS Intensifies the Loop

1. **Very low marginal delivery cost:** A $35 contribution can cover roughly a thousand additional SMS segments before labor, data, or agency fees.
2. **Attribution:** Unique links identify which list, message, sender persona, and urgency framing produced the donation.
3. **Rapid iteration:** Agencies can test copy and ask amounts, then concentrate volume on the most lucrative version.
4. **Reusable donor asset:** A successful donation can place the person's phone number and giving history into future segmentation, subject to committee agreements, privacy policies, and carrier rules.
5. **Self-financing solicitation:** If each round returns slightly more than its marginal acquisition cost, most revenue can be reinvested in acquiring and soliciting donors even when little is left for campaigning.

The economic risk is therefore not that SMS transmission is expensive. It is that cheap, measurable messaging makes **raising the next dollar** an indefinitely scalable activity.

## Reporting Checklist

For any fundraising text, identify:

- The legal committee named on the contribution page and its FEC ID.
- Whether the page sends money to one committee, a joint fundraising committee, or a split slate.
- The payment processor versus the digital agency and SMS platform.
- Gross contributions, refunds/chargebacks, processing fees, vendor payments, list costs, transfers to candidates/parties, independent expenditures, and cash retained.
- Whether a vendor payment is a fee, commission, media buy, list rental, or pass-through payment.
- Net revenue and incremental cost—not merely gross dollars attributed to an SMS link.

Without those distinctions, “the company kept X%” is usually stronger than the public filings support.
