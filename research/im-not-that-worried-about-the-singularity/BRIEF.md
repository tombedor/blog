# Research brief: I'm not that worried about the singularity

**Scope:** Compare the draft's definitions and risk model with other thinkers; source its historical examples. Researched 2026-09-09. Comparisons are analysis, not claims of influence.

## Definition and intellectual alignment

- **Your threshold:** AI performs better without human participation; the later formulation also requires independence from human maintenance. These are distinct thresholds. Neither alone establishes that humans lose control.
- **Closest precedent — [I. J. Good (1965, pp. 33–34)](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/1966-good.pdf):** anticipates human–machine collaboration leading until ultraintelligent machines arrive. His central mechanism is machines designing better machines; yours is humans ceasing to contribute.
- **[Vinge (1993)](https://frc.ri.cmu.edu/~hpm/book98/com.ch1/vinge.singularity.html) / [Kurzweil (2011)](https://www.writingsbyraykurzweil.com/so-what-is-the-singularity):** include human augmentation or merger. Your autonomous-AI threshold excludes some futures they count as singularities. **[Chalmers (2010, pp. 1–3)](https://www.consc.net/papers/singularity.pdf):** recursive intelligence explosion; your definition requires neither recursion nor sudden acceleration.
- **Closest broader framework — [Drexler (2019; 2023 update)](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/LxNwBNxXktvzAko65/reframing-superintelligence-llms-4-years):** intelligence supplied through services and organizations; superhuman capability need not imply an independent agent. He explicitly retains concern about dangerous agents.
- **Strongest challenges — [Bostrom (2019)](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/vulnerable.pdf):** technology can make destruction too accessible *before* autonomous AI. **[Omohundro (2008)](https://steveomohundro.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/12/ai_drives_final.pdf) / [Bostrom (2012)](https://nickbostrom.com/superintelligentwill.pdf):** goal-directed systems can instrumentally resist shutdown without biological drives or consciousness. These are conditional arguments, not findings that every model wants survival.

## Attackers and defenders: most useful reading

- **[Garfinkel & Dafoe (2019)](https://warontherocks.com/artificial-intelligence-foresight-and-the-offense-defense-balance/):** closest match to your abundance argument. Their “offensive-then-defensive scaling” allows equal capability growth to favor attackers initially, defenders later. Equal tools do not imply an unchanged balance.
- **[Shevlane & Dafoe (2020, §§4–6)](https://www.aies-conference.com/2020/wp-content/papers/117.pdf):** strongest support for your patching argument. One deployed fix can close a vulnerability broadly; social manipulation is much harder to patch.
- **[Schneier (2018)](https://www.schneier.com/essays/archives/2018/03/artificial_intellige.html):** explicitly shares defensive optimism: “AI has the capability to tip the scales more toward defense.” **[Lohn (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13371):** counters that cyber is too varied for one aggregate verdict.
- **[Jervis (1978, pp. 186–214)](https://www.sfu.ca/~kawasaki/Jervis%20Cooperation.pdf):** distinguish offense–defense advantage from whether defensive preparations *look offensive*. **[SIPRI (2025)](https://www.sipri.org/publications/2025/sipri-insights-peace-and-security/impact-military-artificial-intelligence-nuclear-escalation-risk):** AI can increase escalation through compressed decisions and threatened retaliation, without hacking launch systems.

## Historical references and evidence checks

- **Nuclear close calls:** [B-59 / Arkhipov, 27 October 1962](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/russia-programs/2022-10-03/soviet-submarines-nuclear-torpedoes-cuban-missile-crisis) is probably the submarine reference; it belongs to the Cuban Missile Crisis. [NORAD, 1979–80](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/nukevault/ebb371/) supplies separate technical-error examples. Neither proves extinction was inevitable.
- **Weapons:** machine guns contributed to defensive strength, but also supported assaults; cheap drone strikes do not automatically imply easier conquest. [Historical evidence and qualifications](historical-events.md).
- **Internet:** documented examples cover voting effects, Pizzagate violence, and newspaper job losses; they do not establish “destroyed shared truth” or that misinformation decided an election. [Citations](historical-events.md#internet).
- **Chrome:** [Google, 30 July 2026](https://blog.google/security/chrome-stronger-with-every-update/) reports **1,072 security bugs fixed in Chrome 149–150**, exceeding the previous 23 milestones combined. Candidate AI fixes still undergo review; this measures remediation throughput, not net cyber safety.
