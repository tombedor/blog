# Historical references and citation scope

Researched 2026-09-09. “Candidate example” means the draft describes a category without identifying a particular event; it does not mean this was necessarily the author's intended reference.

## Nuclear close calls

| Draft reference | Citation and established event | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cuban Missile Crisis | [U.S. Office of the Historian, “The Cuban Missile Crisis, October 1962”](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis). October 1962 confrontation over Soviet missiles in Cuba; the 27 October U-2 shootdown increased pressure during negotiations. | Evidence of escalation and miscommunication, not a quantified probability of extinction. The central agreement on 28 October did not immediately end every operational danger. |
| “Submarine thing” — likely identification | [National Security Archive, “The Underwater Cuban Missile Crisis at 60” (2022)](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/briefing-book/russia-programs/2022-10-03/soviet-submarines-nuclear-torpedoes-cuban-missile-crisis). B-59, 27 October 1962; publishes Arkhipov's 1997 recollection alongside other records. Arkhipov helped avoid a potentially catastrophic nuclear-torpedo decision. | This was part of the same crisis. The archive warns about sensationalized accounts; do not present a simple, fully documented three-person vote or inevitable ensuing world war as settled fact. |
| “Sheer error” — separate candidate | [National Security Archive, “The 3 A.M. Phone Call” (2012)](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/nukevault/ebb371/). November 1979 and June 1980 false attack warnings involved an exercise scenario and faulty computer components, prompting military alert actions. | A clearer technical-error example than B-59. False warning and alert are not equivalent to a nuclear launch; checking and human safeguards mattered. |

**Citation provenance:** The National Security Archive is a nongovernmental archive at George Washington University, not the U.S. National Security Agency. Its collections link to declassified records and participant testimony; those underlying documents are preferable for unusually precise claims about procedures or individual decisions.

## Nuclear weapons, deterrence, and “destroy humanity”

[Scott Sagan and Kenneth Waltz, *The Spread of Nuclear Weapons: A Debate* (1995), institutional publication description](https://cisac.fsi.stanford.edu/publications/the_spread_of_nuclear_weapons_a_debate).

- The draft's deterrence argument resembles Waltz's: higher costs of war may stabilize relations. Sagan emphasizes organizational limitations on safe, rational control.
- This is a useful paired reading precisely because the causal claim is contested. Absence of all-out superpower war does not isolate the contribution of nuclear weapons, and “discouraged” is more defensible than asserting they eliminated war.
- Access here was to the institutional book description, not the complete book; no page-specific quotation is supplied.

[Lili Xia et al., “Global food insecurity and famine…” (*Nature Food*, 2022)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43016-022-00573-0).

- Models global food effects of nuclear-war soot scenarios; estimates more than five billion deaths in a U.S.–Russia war scenario.
- Supports catastrophic global consequences. It does not demonstrate literal human extinction or show that such destructive capacity already existed throughout both world wars.
- “Since the world wars” collapses conventional industrial warfare, atomic weapons, and later arsenals into one category. The historical and modeled claims need to be separated.
- “A few people decide” also differs from “a few people can independently build, maintain, and execute the entire system.” This is an analytical qualification to the coordination-cost argument.

## Machine guns and stalemate

[Imperial War Museums, First World War gallery guide](https://www.iwm.org.uk/sites/default/files/files/2023-10/first_world_war_large_print_guide.pdf), entries on the Machine Gun Corps and Lewis gun; [Garfinkel and Dafoe's historical framing](https://warontherocks.com/artificial-intelligence-foresight-and-the-offense-defense-balance/).

- Machine guns are a reasonable illustration of defensive firepower, particularly in combination with barbed wire and entrenched positions.
- The museum also describes offensive uses: Vickers guns contributed supporting fire, while portable Lewis guns enabled advancing platoons. A weapon's role changes with organization and tactics.
- “The machine gun caused stalemate” is a stronger monocausal claim than this evidence supports. Scope the example to its contribution to defensive strength on the Western Front.
- Access limitation: the museum PDF's relevant passages were available through indexed text, but the full PDF could not be opened by the browser tool. No unverified page number is supplied.

## Drones: distinguish strikes from strategic offense

[RUSI, *Emergent Approaches to Combined Arms Manoeuvre in Ukraine* (October 2025)](https://www.rusi.org/explore-our-research/publications/insights-papers/emergent-approaches-combined-arms-manoeuvre-ukraine), sections on Ukrainian battlefield geometry, UAVs, and armour; based partly on field observation.

- Documents extensive drone employment alongside logistics adaptation, protected mobility, artillery, and armour. Some observed tanks continued operating despite repeated hits, making recovery and repair relevant to effectiveness.
- Supports treating drones as part of a changing combined system, rather than assuming every cheap hit destroys a valuable platform.
- Analytical correction: both an advancing and a defending force have supply lines. Disrupting logistics can support either holding territory or capturing it. Tactical strike effectiveness and campaign-level offense advantage are different questions.
- Findings concern particular battlefield conditions and observations, not a timeless verdict about drones.

## Internet

### Wider information access: a concrete enabling event

[CERN, public release of the Web, 30 April 1993](https://timeline.web.cern.ch/cern-puts-world-wide-web-public-domain).

- CERN put Web software into the public domain, supporting its open adoption. A concrete institutional event behind wider access, not a measure proving that everyone benefited equally.
- Distinguish the Web from the Internet: the Internet already existed.

### “Swayed elections”: separate behavior from election outcomes

[Robert Bond et al., “A 61-million-person experiment in social influence and political mobilization” (*Nature*, 2012)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11421).

- Randomized Facebook mobilization messages during the 2010 U.S. congressional elections affected real-world voting behavior. A stronger causal citation for electoral influence than anecdotal claims about persuasion.
- It is about turnout and social influence; it does not show a particular candidate won because of misinformation. Accessible evidence here was the paper's abstract; no additional numerical effect is inferred.

[Hunt Allcott and Matthew Gentzkow, “Social Media and Fake News in the 2016 Election” (2017)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23089).

- Documents exposure to fabricated stories and greater reported belief in congenial stories. Useful for misinformation circulation and partisan reception.
- Exposure and sharing counts do not establish persuasion sufficient to reverse the election result. “Destroyed shared notions of truth” remains an interpretive generalization, not a fact this study demonstrates.

### Online conspiracy beliefs and violence: candidate example

[U.S. DOJ, Edgar Maddison Welch sentencing announcement, 22 June 2017](https://www.justice.gov/usao-dc/pr/north-carolina-man-sentenced-four-year-prison-term-armed-assault-northwest-washington).

- On 4 December 2016, Welch brought firearms into Comet Ping Pong and fired a rifle; the government's account identifies unfounded child-trafficking rumors as at least part of his motivation. He received a four-year sentence.
- Supports an individual instance of conspiracy-driven violence. Nobody was injured in the shooting; do not imply deaths in this example.

### Journalism: employment evidence, not a prestige measure

[Pew Research Center, “U.S. newsroom employment has fallen 26% since 2008” (13 July 2021)](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/07/13/u-s-newsroom-employment-has-fallen-26-since-2008/).

- Analysis of U.S. labor data: newspaper newsroom employment declined 57%, approximately 71,000 to 31,000 jobs, from 2008 to 2020. Digital-native employment grew during the same period.
- Supports severe disruption, with a clearly delimited place, sector, and period. Does not establish the disappearance of journalism, a collapse in prestige, or a single Internet-only cause.

### Online harassment: prevalence evidence

[Emily A. Vogels / Pew Research Center, “The State of Online Harassment” (13 January 2021)](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2021/01/13/the-state-of-online-harassment/).

- In a September 2020 survey of 10,093 U.S. adults, 41% reported at least one measured form of online harassment; 25% reported more severe forms, including threats, stalking, sexual harassment, or sustained harassment.
- Supports widespread online harassment. It does not directly measure cross-border perpetrators or establish literal access by anyone to anyone worldwide.
