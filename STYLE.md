# Style guide

## Principles

- Prefer the shortest version that preserves meaning, qualification, and voice.
- Spend words on distinctions, examples, and consequences—not emphasis or throat-clearing.
- Prefer concrete subjects and strong verbs.
- Long sentences are welcome when their structure clarifies relationships.
- A qualification should change the claim. Otherwise, remove it.
- Use the same term for the same concept. Do not vary terminology merely for variety.

## Review flags

These words are not prohibited. When they appear, ask whether the sentence explains what they mean.

- **difficult, easy:** Difficult in what way—slow, complex, risky, tedious, or uncertain?
- **important, significant:** What changes because of it?
- **better, worse, effective:** By what measure?
- **interesting:** What is surprising, revealing, or contradictory?
- **often, usually, quickly:** Compared with what, or over what period?
- **things, stuff, aspects, various:** Name the objects or categories.
- **seems, feels:** Is this uncertainty, perception, or unsupported judgment?
- **I think, I believe:** Keep only when the degree of confidence matters.
- **there is, there are, it is:** Check whether these constructions hide the actor.
- **this, that:** Supply a noun when the reference could be ambiguous.
- **make, do, have, provide:** Look for a more descriptive verb.
- **somehow:** Explain the mechanism or acknowledge that it is unknown.

## Compression checks

- Remove intensifiers before strengthening the underlying description.
- Replace a weak verb plus noun with one verb:
  - “make a decision” → “decide”
  - “provide an explanation” → “explain”
- Do not hedge the same claim twice.
- Cut opening clauses that merely announce the sentence:
  - “It is worth noting that…”
  - “The fact of the matter is…”
  - “What is interesting is…”
- If two adjacent sentences make the same point, keep the more concrete one.

## Rhythm

- Vary sentence length.
- Use short sentences for conclusions, reversals, and emphasis.
- Use longer sentences when several qualifications belong to one thought.
- Read paragraphs aloud. Rewrite places where the syntax obscures the emphasis.

## Banned phrases

Reserve this section for personal habits that have repeatedly produced weak sentences, not every construction that can be misused.

- Avoid empty intensifiers: _really_, _extremely_, _very_.
- **“It's difficult”:** Describe what is difficult.
- **“In this way”:** Be explicit.
